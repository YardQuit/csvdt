Splits a single rfc3339 timestamp in the given column [0...]
into two separate columns: date and time. The date is written as
YYYY-MM-DD, the time as HH:MM:SS followed by the offset -- e.g.
2024-06-15 and 14:00:00 +02:00. Both read from the timestamp's
own offset rather than from UTC, so the date is the one it was
where the record was written, not the date the same instant had
in UTC.

Sub-second precision is kept when the timestamp carries it, as
14:00:00.500 +02:00, on the same terms as every other conversion
here: what the source had is what comes out. The time column is
a time of day with an offset, not an RFC3339 timestamp, so csvdt
does not read it back as one. Keep the original column, or use
-i before/after rather than replace, if the timestamp is still
needed.
