Reorders the columns of the CSV output. Takes the columns [0...]
you want first, comma-joined with no space, in the order you
want them (e.g. -a3,1,0,2). Columns you don't name are not
dropped: they follow the ones you did, keeping their original
relative order. So on a five-column file -a3,1 gives
3,1,0,2,4 -- which means moving one column to the front is just
-a3, without having to list the rest.

The row always comes out the same width it went in. Use --remove
to drop columns, and combine the two by piping if you want both.
A column may appear at most once, since the list is the new
order. Every column named must exist, or the row is reported as
out of bound.
